Judge development log: Qwen3.8-27B as a scorer for generated robot videos
===========================================================================

Setting
-------
Judge: Qwen/Qwen3.8-27B loaded 4-bit (bitsandbytes NF4) on one RTX 4090, greedy decoding,
thinking off. Script: scripts/judge_videos.py. Calibration set: the first 4 train clips x 4 seeds
= 16 rollouts from the base Cosmos3-Edge (clips 000015, 000036, 000062, 000165), hand-scored
from 8-frame contact sheets on the same four 1-5 axes (outputs/rollouts/train/manual_scores_v1.json).

Every variant below was run on the same 16 videos. Agreement is reported as bias / mean absolute
error against the hand scores per axis, and as "seed-order": within-clip pairwise ordering
agreement of total score, which is the quantity preference-pair construction depends on.

  variant            task        interact    physical    temporal   seed-order  latency
  v1 2 frames      -0.31/1.44  -0.56/1.56  +1.88/1.88  +1.00/1.00    9/13       21 s
  v2 video 512     +0.69/1.06  +0.56/1.06  +1.19/1.44  +0.69/0.94   12/20       43 s
  v3 +mid frame    +0.75/1.00  +0.75/1.00  +0.94/1.31  +0.38/0.88   12/20       48 s
  v4 frame images  +0.06/0.69  -0.25/0.88  +0.25/0.88   0.00/0.88   16/22       51 s

v4 is the shipped default.


Problem 1: the judge only saw two frames
----------------------------------------
Symptom. v1 gave physical consistency 4-5 to almost everything with reasons like "all objects
remain present", on clips where most beads vanish after the first second. Its observations in v2
said "across the two frames".

Cause. Qwen3-VL's video processor assumes a 24 fps source when handed a pre-sampled list of frames.
16 frames were interpreted as a 0.67 s clip and resampled to 2 frames at 0.1 s and 0.5 s
(video_grid_thw [2,16,16]). Confirmed with a CPU-only processor test. The transformers warning
about missing video_metadata is easy to miss in the load noise.

Fix. Pass real timing metadata and disable resampling:
  processor(text=[...], videos=[frames_uint8],
            videos_kwargs={"video_metadata": [{"fps", "total_num_frames", "duration",
                                               "frames_indices"}],
                           "do_sample_frames": False})
This yields grid_thw [8,32,32] for 16 frames at 512 px with timestamps 0.6 s ... 16.9 s. The script
now raises if the processor keeps fewer frames than it was given.


Problem 2: video mode cannot see small objects
----------------------------------------------
Symptom. With all 16 frames delivered (v2, v3), the judge caught single large objects appearing or
vanishing (a blue block materialising, a lemon disappearing from a pot, a lemon teleporting) but
still reported "no objects vanish" on the bead-scraping clip where the tray goes from ~60 beads to
nearly empty.

Cause. In video mode Qwen merges each pair of frames (temporal patch 2) and each 2x2 patch block
into one token. A field of beads a few pixels wide becomes texture.

Fix. Send each frame as a separately labelled image ("Frame k of 16 (t = .. s):") so every frame
keeps its own 32x32 token grid with no temporal merging (~4.4k visual tokens per video instead of
~2.1k). This was the single largest improvement: bias fell to about zero on every axis and the
judge's anomalies field started reporting "individual beads disappear and are replaced by a single
solid object" and "beads cluster into a pile without any visible contact". Frames are upscaled to
512 px first so one token covers 16 px of the original 256 px frame.


Problem 3: loose rubric
-----------------------
The first prompt gave one-line definitions and "1 = very bad, 5 = excellent". Replaced with
anchored scales: each axis states what earns each of the five scores in checkable terms
(e.g. physical consistency 3 = exactly one clear violation such as one object disappearing;
2 = two or three violations or the manipulated object vanishing). Added decision rules: "touching"
means the object is between or against the fingers, not merely below the gripper; when torn
between two scores give the lower; a 5 requires no flaw found on that axis.


Problem 4: scores not grounded in evidence
------------------------------------------
Added a mandatory observations block that the judge must fill before any score:
  frame_1_objects, middle_frame_objects, last_frame_objects   (with approximate counts)
  gripper_contact   (which frames, which object)
  anomalies         (vanish / appear / duplicate / morph / teleport)
with a hard rule: a count change over ~20%, an object missing in any later frame, or a new object
caps physical consistency at 3 (2 if it is the manipulated object or many items vanished).
Objects that vanish and later reappear count as a violation even if first and last frames match
(this is why the middle frame was added in v3; on its own it did not help because of Problem 2).
The observations are stored per video, so the judge can be audited on what it saw, not only on
the final number.


What still disagrees with the hand scores (v4)
----------------------------------------------
- Task credit for a related but not the described action: clip 000062 seed 2 moves the lemon into
  the pot cleanly and gets task 4 / interaction 5; the caption says the arm lifts the pot first.
  The judge rewards the visible manipulation, the hand score rewards following the caption.
- One bead clip (000015 seed 0) still scored physical 5 where the hand score was 2 for mid-clip
  thinning. The judge counted 40-50 beads at all three checkpoints.
- Where the judge is harsher than the hand score it is usually defensible on inspection (e.g. rods
  "grasped" without moving). These are rubric judgment calls, not perception failures.


Cost and next step
------------------
~51 s per video unbatched (4-bit, bnb dequant on every forward, causal-conv1d fallback kernel).
4,888 rollouts would take ~69 GPU-hours. Options in order of effort: left-padded batched generation
(--batch-size, being measured), serving a pre-quantized AWQ/INT4 checkpoint with vLLM from the
framework's venv, or a second GPU with --shard.

Before the judge's scores are used for training, agreement should be re-measured on a larger
held-out set labelled independently (the plan's "VLM-human agreement" metric), not on these 16
videos that were used to tune the prompt.
